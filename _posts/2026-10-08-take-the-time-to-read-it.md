Lately, I've been noticing more and more often that focused attention and a conscious effort
to really understand something are truly rewarded when it comes to reading just about anything.
So often I stumble upon a peace of documentation looking for something specific
that is "probably down there somewhere if I just scroll down more".

<!-- more -->

What usually happens is I find what I'm looking for and leave the page immediately.
What only seldom happens is I remind myself to be patient, start reading from the beginning
and learn something very valuable and generally usable.
It's almost always like "ha, I found the ripest piece of all the fruit on this tree, well done, let's leave"
when what atually happened is I just walked past baskets of beautiful, ripe fruit,
prepared with care, free, there for the taking.

Things like how to frame a certain topic or a question. Or what the right way is to think
about a certain problem space and how professionals working in the field think about it.
A good example is reading MySql or PostgreSQL documentation. I always learn something I wasn't
looking for when visiting those docs.
Or basically any good software with high quality documentation that starts off by
detailing the problem space that the software is there to solve.

These parts are not the piece of information we are looking for but they can teach us much
more than just that one bit that we needed.

Take the time to read them.
